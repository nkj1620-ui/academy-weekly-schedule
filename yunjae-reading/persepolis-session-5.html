IN CLASS · 30 MINUTES

Class presentation

01 / 15

Recall

In chapter 4, adults and children read the same event differently. Recall one moment when Marji missed something an adult understood.

02 / 15

Meet Mehri

Mehri grew up working in Marji’s home. What does Marji mean when she thinks of her as a sister?

03 / 15

Notice a feeling

Marji feels uneasy about her father’s Cadillac. How can a car tell us something about a family’s place in society?

04 / 15

Who writes?

Mehri falls for the neighbor but cannot write to him herself. Name the person holding the pen and the person choosing what to say.

05 / 15

Who speaks?

If Marji writes a sentence that Mehri dictates, whose voice would the neighbor hear as he reads it? Defend your answer.

06 / 15

Trace the message

Follow a letter from Mehri to the neighbor. Where does Marji make communication possible?

07 / 15

Find the turning point

What new information does Marji’s father give the neighbor? What does the neighbor do with the letters afterward?

08 / 15

Test an explanation

The neighbor has already read Mehri’s words. What changes his response if the words themselves stay the same?

09 / 15

Listen to the father

Does Marji’s father present class divisions as his own wish, a social rule, or both? Find the words that shape your answer.

10 / 15

Look closely

Find the panel where Marji lies beside Mehri. Describe their positions before interpreting what their closeness means.

11 / 15

Compare two choices

The neighbor returns the letters. Marji stays with Mehri. What different ideas about belonging do the two actions express?

12 / 15

Follow the protest

Why do Mehri and Marji decide to demonstrate together? Connect their decision to an earlier moment at home.

13 / 15

Read the ending

Their mother learns where they have been on Black Friday. What does she know about the danger that the girls did not know?

14 / 15

Discuss

Marji can offer comfort and can write for Mehri. Which part of Mehri’s situation is beyond Marji’s power to change?

15 / 15

Exit ticket

In two sentences, connect one detail from the letters to one detail from the bedside panel. What does each reveal about class?

Reading comprehension & visual analysis

1. Why does Mehri need Marji’s help?

Model answer

Mehri cannot write the letters herself. Marji can put Mehri’s words on paper.

2. How does the Cadillac connect to Mehri’s story?

Model answer

The car makes the family’s advantages visible. Mehri’s story shows how such differences affect a relationship.

3. Who is the author of the letters in an important sense?

Model answer

Mehri supplies the feelings and message even though Marji supplies the handwriting.

4. What does the father tell the neighbor?

Model answer

He explains that Mehri works for the family. The neighbor had understood her position differently.

5. Why does returning the letters matter?

Model answer

The same letters once helped the couple grow closer. Returning them marks the neighbor’s rejection.

6. What does the father’s explanation ask Marji to accept?

Model answer

He treats marriage within one’s social class as an established rule. Marji questions its fairness.

7. What can you infer from the bedside panel?

Model answer

Marji chooses to be close to Mehri after the rejection. Their physical closeness suggests affection across the class divide.

8. How does the demonstration connect to the letters?

Model answer

An unfair division that Marji sees at home gives her a personal reason to protest.

9. Why is the timing of their protest important?

Model answer

They go out on Black Friday without understanding the danger their mother later recognizes.

10. Does Marji solve Mehri’s problem?

Model answer

She makes communication possible and offers comfort. She cannot determine the neighbor’s choice or undo the social rule.

Critical thinking · Discuss

Marji can offer comfort and can write for Mehri. Which part of Mehri’s situation is beyond Marji’s power to change?

Try a second interpretation. Which detail in the chapter helps you decide between the two?
