IN-CLASS · 15 SLIDES · 30 MIN

Class presentation

01 · RECAP

From The Bicycle to The Water Cell

Last class: protests, collective action, and Marji's desire to join the revolution. Today: how political history enters a family story.

02 · ESSENTIAL QUESTION

Who gets to tell history?

How does Marji's understanding change when she learns what happened to her own grandfather?

03 · CONTEXT

The Shah and the coup

Her father challenges the simple story she learned about the Shah. Distinguish a ruler's claim to power from the historical process by which power was gained.

04 · SOURCE CHECK

Teacher vs. father

Compare what Marji says she learned at school with what her father tells her. What might each speaker know, believe, or leave out?

05 · FAMILY HISTORY

A surprising discovery

Marji finds out that the deposed emperor was her great-grandfather. At first she is delighted by her connection to royalty.

06 · CHANGE OVER TIME

Prince → prime minister → prisoner

Her grandfather loses his privileged position, encounters new political ideas, and is repeatedly imprisoned for opposing the regime.

07 · KEY TERM

What does communist mean?

In this chapter, it refers to a political belief associated with class equality and common ownership. Avoid treating all communist movements as identical.

08 · FAMILY IMPACT

Politics is personal

Marji's mother remembers fearing arrests and seeing the suffering of her father. What does the private family perspective add?

09 · THE WATER CELL

A deliberate form of suffering

The prison authorities kept her grandfather in a water-filled cell for extended periods. Political persecution becomes a physical experience.

10 · VISUAL FOCUS

Look at the bath sequence

In your copy, find Marji in the bathtub. Track composition, facial expression, space, and the contrast between the bath and prison.

11 · SYMBOLISM

What can water mean?

Water can suggest everyday comfort in Marji's home but deliberate suffering in the prison. Explain the contrast using a specific panel.

12 · ETHICAL THINKING

Can we reproduce suffering?

Marji tries to understand her grandfather through her own bath. Does this show empathy, limited understanding, or both?

13 · CLAIM + EVIDENCE

Move beyond summary

Claim: The chapter turns distant history into personal family memory. Evidence: Marji learns about her grandfather's imprisonment and tries to imagine his experience.

14 · DISCUSSION

Two voices, one history

How do a child's curiosity and the adults' painful memories create different views of the same events?

15 · EXIT TICKET

One image, one insight

In 2–3 sentences, explain why the bathtub scene matters. Include a precise visual or narrative detail from your book.

Reading comprehension & visual analysis

Answer using evidence from “The Water Cell.” Open “Model answer” only after attempting your own answer.

1. Why does Marji initially believe the Shah's rule is right?

Model answer: She repeats an official explanation taught by authority figures, before her father offers a different historical account.

2. What is the important difference between a claim of divine authority and a political explanation?

Model answer: A claim of divine authority presents rule as sacred; her father's account asks how political power was actually gained.

3. What surprises Marji about the deposed emperor?

Model answer: She learns he was her great-grandfather, which connects political history to her family.

4. How does her grandfather's position change?

Model answer: He moves from a privileged background into government service, develops opposing political beliefs, and becomes a prisoner.

5. How does Marji's mother describe the effect of imprisonment?

Model answer: She recalls fear, repeated arrests, and the pain and declining health her father suffered.

6. Why is the water cell more than a historical detail?

Model answer: It makes state violence concrete and bodily, especially for Marji's family.

7. What does Marji try to accomplish by staying in the bathtub?

Model answer: She attempts to imagine her grandfather's experience through an experience of her own.

8. What makes the bathtub an imperfect comparison?

Model answer: Marji can leave her bath and is safe; her grandfather was forcibly confined and harmed.

9. What might the contrast between ordinary water and the prison water symbolize?

Model answer: The same material can suggest comfort in one setting and state-inflicted suffering in another.

10. How does the chapter change the reader's view of revolution?

Model answer: It shifts the focus from abstract public protest to personal loss and memories within one family.

Critical thinking · Discuss

History and authority

When an official textbook and a family memory disagree, how should a careful reader evaluate the claims? What other evidence would be useful?

Empathy and limits

Marji wants to understand suffering. Is her attempt respectful, naive, meaningful, or a combination? Defend your interpretation with visual evidence.
