Success criteria

  2. Distinguish events from people's interpretations.
  3. Explain irony using narrative and visual evidence from a panel.
15 SLIDES · INTERACTIVE PRESENTATION

Class slides

01 / REVIEW

The Water Cell → Persepolis

The last chapter revealed the grandfather’s suffering. This chapter asks how a nation's public history shapes private memory.

02 / QUESTION

Who controls the meaning of a revolution?

Consider rulers, families, protesters, photographs, and the crowd.

03 / GRANDMOTHER

A witness to hardship

Marji's grandmother describes how the family's loss of privilege brought real poverty and difficult choices.

04 / CAUSE & EFFECT

From political change to everyday hunger

How did the change in government affect Marji's grandmother? Separate historical cause from personal consequence.

05 / SYMBOL

Why name the chapter Persepolis?

The ancient Persian capital stands for Iran's long history, which rulers may invoke to strengthen their political legitimacy.

06 / HISTORY

Political spectacle

Grandmother discusses the Shah's lavish celebrations. Ask who benefits when a ruler uses history and wealth to display power.

07 / CONTRAST

Power and poverty

What effect does Satrapi create by placing extravagant royal celebrations beside memories of family hardship?

08 / RISK

Marji's father is late

He has been photographing demonstrations. His family fears that something has happened to him.

09 / VISUAL LITERACY

A photograph as evidence

Unlike a speech, an image records a specific moment—but framing, selection, and interpretation still matter.

10 / THE HOSPITAL

The crowd carries a dead man

Marji's father reports that the crowd recognizes the dead as martyrs of the protest movement.

11 / IRONY

Not every death has the same cause

One man's death was unrelated to the military crackdown. The crowd nonetheless transforms him into a political symbol.

12 / KEY TERM

What makes a martyr?

A martyr is a person remembered as having died for a cause or belief. How might the crowd apply that label too quickly?

13 / TONE

Why do the adults laugh?

The scene combines grief, political anger, and absurdity. Analyze the uneasy humor instead of simply calling it funny.

14 / READER'S ROLE

Belief versus evidence

Compare what actually happened with what protesters believe happened. Why does that distinction matter?

15 / EXIT TICKET

Memory, power, and truth

Using one detail from grandmother's story and one from the hospital, explain how people create meaning from history.

Reading comprehension · 10 questions

Write your answer before opening the suggested model answer. Include evidence from a specific panel in your own copy.

1. What does the grandmother reveal about the family's earlier life?
Show model answer

Her family became poor after political changes stripped away privilege; her memories make historical events tangible.

2. What is the significance of the chapter title?
Show model answer

Persepolis evokes ancient Persian history and the way governments can use historical grandeur to justify present authority.

3. How do the lavish celebrations contrast with the grandmother's experience?
Show model answer

The rulers display luxury while the grandmother remembers deprivation, highlighting unequal distributions of power and wealth.

4. Why does Marji's family worry about her father?
Show model answer

He is late returning from photographing protests, a dangerous activity under political repression.

5. What role does photography play in this chapter?
Show model answer

It functions as a way to document political unrest, though images still require careful interpretation.

6. What did Marji's father see outside the hospital?
Show model answer

A crowd was carrying bodies and treating the deceased as martyrs of the protest movement.

7. Why is one man's death ironic?
Show model answer

He died from cancer, rather than being killed by the authorities, yet the crowd claims him as a martyr.

8. How does the widow respond?
Show model answer

She joins in the public reaction, illustrating how personal grief and collective political feeling can become entangled.

9. How does Satrapi mix serious and humorous elements?
Show model answer

The risk of protest and genuine deaths are serious, while the mistaken attribution produces unsettling irony.

10. What does the chapter suggest about collective narratives?
Show model answer

Groups may turn events into powerful symbols before checking the evidence, especially during moments of unrest.

Critical thinking discussion

Public image vs. private truth

How can a leader use a nation’s ancient history to make a present government appear legitimate? How might those living in poverty respond?

Photographs and interpretation

A photograph can show a body being carried, but can it prove how the person died? What additional evidence would be needed?
