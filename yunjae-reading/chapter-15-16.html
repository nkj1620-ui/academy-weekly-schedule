03 / COMPREHENSION

10 Reading Questions

Answer in complete sentences. Include a specific story detail when possible. Open the teacher answer only after attempting your response.

1. Why is Nya unable to drink water from the new well yet?
Teacher's model answer

The water is still muddy from drilling; the team must reach clean groundwater and complete the pump installation.

2. What must the workers do before the well can be used?
Teacher's model answer

They must drill deeper, install pipes and a pump, prepare the foundation, and let cement dry.

3. What does Nya do even after water first appears?
Teacher's model answer

She makes another trip to the pond because the well is not ready.

4. Why does Salva go to Nairobi?
Teacher's model answer

He must complete the procedures needed before resettling in the United States.

5. What does the airplane drink remind Salva of?
Teacher's model answer

It brings back memories of his father and happy moments with his family.

6. Why does Salva feel emotional when he arrives in Rochester?
Teacher's model answer

He is entering a new life and feels both the welcome of his new family and the pain of leaving his old life behind.

7. What are the villagers preparing in Chapter 16?
Teacher's model answer

They clear land for a new building. Its purpose is left as a question in this chapter.

8. How does Salva begin to adjust to life in Rochester?
Teacher's model answer

He studies English and plays volleyball, gradually building skills and relationships.

9. What long-term goal begins to form while Salva studies?
Teacher's model answer

He wants to return to Sudan and help the people there.

10. What important news changes Salva's plans?
Teacher's model answer

His cousin reports that his father is alive at a UN clinic, leading Salva to travel back to Sudan.

04 / WRITING AND DISCUSSION

Evidence-Based Paragraph

Prompt: In Chapters 15–16, how does a new opportunity bring both hope and uncertainty to Salva or Nya?
  1. Make a claim about one character.
  2. Describe two accurate details from Chapters 15–16.
  3. Explain how each detail supports your claim.
  4. End with a thoughtful concluding sentence.

Length: 6–8 sentences. Useful frames: “At first, …”; “For example, …”; “This suggests that …”; “Although …, …”; “Therefore, …”

Compare the timelines

Explain one similarity and one difference between Nya's changing village and Salva's changing life. Use the words transition and possibility accurately.

Teacher Model (open after writing)

View model paragraph

In Chapters 15–16, Salva's new opportunities bring hope and uncertainty at the same time. After arriving in Rochester, he is welcomed into a new family, but he also misses the life and people he left behind. Learning English is difficult, yet he continues studying and joins a volleyball team. Over time, his education allows him to imagine helping people in Sudan. When his cousin sends news about his father, Salva gains new hope but cannot be certain what he will find. These events show that a new beginning does not erase the past. Instead, Salva moves forward while continuing to care about his family and homeland.
