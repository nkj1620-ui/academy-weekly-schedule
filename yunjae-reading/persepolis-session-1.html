CONTEXT · READ BEFORE CLASS

A small amount of background

Place & time

Iran is a country in Western Asia; Tehran is its capital. The Shah was Iran’s monarch. The 1979 revolution ended his rule and was followed by the establishment of an Islamic Republic. The opening school scene takes place in 1980.

The story moves between this opening and earlier memories. A flashback is a move to an earlier time.

Whose story?

A memoir tells remembered experiences from the author’s life. A graphic memoir combines words and sequential images. Marjane Satrapi looks back on her younger self, Marji.

Distinguish what the child understands from what the adult storyteller helps the reader notice. One family’s experience does not represent every Iranian person.

Reading tools: panel = one framed image; caption = narration; speech bubble = a character’s spoken words.

03 · UNDERSTAND THE STORY

Reading check

Answer aloud in class. Record keywords if helpful.

1. The school

What two changes affect Marji’s school life in The Veil? How do the children respond to the veil?

2. Her ambition

Why does Marji want to become a prophet? Identify one problem she wants to solve.

3. Taking part

After hearing about the cinema fire in The Bicycle, what does Marji want to do? Why do her parents refuse?

Look closely: children and the veil

Find in your book: the early sequence in The Veil where the children play with their veils. Keep the page open. No copied panels are needed.

  1. Notice: describe a gesture, expression, or way the veil is used. State what you actually see.
  2. Infer: what might this suggest about the children’s understanding of the rule?
  3. Connect: how do the picture and nearby narration work together?

A visual detail is evidence. “The image is interesting” is a reaction; explain exactly what makes it meaningful.

Discuss: two questions

1. Why a bicycle?

Find the bicycle comparison in Chapter 2. How does the image help explain collective action? What part of a revolution is too complicated for this comparison?

2. What kind of child is Marji?

Is she idealistic, confused, brave, or a mixture? Support your view with one detail from each chapter. Can someone care deeply about justice without fully understanding danger?

Chapter 2 includes deaths in a cinema fire. Discuss its effect on Marji without graphic detail. Distinguish the family’s account within the memoir from independently established historical claims.

Reading check · suggested answers
  1. Veiling becomes compulsory at school; boys and girls are separated. Children play with the veils, showing that their understanding differs from the rule’s adult significance.
  2. She wants to address suffering and unfairness. Examples include her grandmother’s pain or the maid’s separation from family meals. Accept a relevant detail supported by the student’s copy.
  3. She wants to join the demonstration. Her parents consider her too young and the situation too dangerous.
Visual analysis & discussion · possible directions

Veil sequence: playful gestures or unconventional uses of the veil may suggest confusion, imagination, or limited understanding. Ask the student to locate the exact panel. Avoid assuming all children have identical feelings.

Bicycle: continued movement can suggest the effort needed to sustain a revolution. The comparison makes an abstract idea concrete, but it cannot fully explain competing goals, violence, or what happens after a government falls.

Character: her desire to help others supports “idealistic”; her wish to demonstrate supports commitment. “Brave” needs explanation because wanting to participate is not the same as understanding the risk. Accept a mixed interpretation.

Sample paragraph & quick feedback rubric

One possible response, not the only answer: Marji wants to make the world fairer, but adults control many of the choices around her. In The Veil, she imagines a religious role that would let her help people who suffer. Yet the children’s playful use of their veils suggests that a rule can affect them before they understand it. The contrast between their play and the serious rule makes this gap visible. In The Bicycle, Marji wants to join a demonstration, but her parents refuse because of the danger. Both chapters show that caring about change does not give her the freedom or experience to control it.
