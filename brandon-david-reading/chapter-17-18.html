02 / TEXT EVIDENCE

Five passages for close reading

Exact excerpts from the previously supplied PDF. Page numbers refer to the PDF viewer’s 93-page count, not printed book pagination. Summaries, questions, examples, and model answers below are original teaching material.

EXCERPT 1 · CHAPTER 17

Water opens the school door

"Yes," he replied. "With the well here, no one will have to go to the pond anymore. So all the children will be able to go to school."

Linda Sue Park, A Long Walk to Water (2010), Chapter 17 · supplied PDF p. 80.

Notice: Cause and effect: well → less time collecting water → time for school.

Teacher’s guide · open after responding

The well removes the long trip to the pond. Children gain time to attend school. “So” signals a cause-and-effect relationship.

EXCERPT 2 · CHAPTER 17

Hope becomes a method

A step at a time.
One problem at a time—just figure out this one problem.
Day by day, solving one problem at a time, Salva moved toward his goal.

Linda Sue Park, A Long Walk to Water (2010), Chapter 17 · supplied PDF p. 85.

Notice: Repetition and a recurring idea: a survival strategy becomes a leadership strategy.

Teacher’s guide · open after responding

Repetition breaks an overwhelming goal into manageable tasks. Uncle once helped Salva cross the desert in small stages; Salva now uses the same approach to organize a project.

EXCERPT 3 · CHAPTER 18

A different experience of water

The water was delicious. It wasn't warm or muddy, like the water from the pond. It was cool and clear.

Linda Sue Park, A Long Walk to Water (2010), Chapter 18 · supplied PDF p. 87.

Notice: Sensory imagery and contrast: connect word choice to Nya’s relief.

Teacher’s guide · open after responding

“Warm or muddy” recalls the unpleasant pond water, while “cool and clear” makes the new water appealing. The contrast makes a major change tangible through temperature and appearance.

EXCERPT 4 · CHAPTER 18

Beyond an old division

"He has drilled many wells for his own people. This year he decided to drill for the Nuer as well."

Linda Sue Park, A Long Walk to Water (2010), Chapter 18 · supplied PDF p. 89.

Notice: Inference: explain what an action reveals without claiming the wider conflict is over.

Teacher’s guide · open after responding

“As well” expands the people he helps beyond his own group. His action suggests compassion across the Dinka–Nuer division described in the story.

EXCERPT 5 · CHAPTER 18

Two stories meet

"I am happy to meet you, Nya," he said. "My name is Salva."

Linda Sue Park, A Long Walk to Water (2010), Chapter 18 · supplied PDF p. 89.

Notice: Narrative structure: a final revelation connects two lives and two time periods.

Teacher’s guide · open after responding

The name reveals that the boy whose journey we followed is the adult leading the well project. The alternating narratives become one connected story: Salva’s actions change Nya’s future.

05 / DISCUSSION & WRITING

From survival to service

Discussion

  1. How does water represent danger, blessing, and opportunity at different points in the story?
  2. Is the well only a gift, or does it also create new responsibilities? Explain.
  3. What would be lost if the novel told Salva’s story first and Nya’s story afterward?

Evidence-based paragraph · 6–8 sentences

Prompt: How does the ending show that perseverance can become service to others?

  1. State a claim about Salva’s transformation.
  2. Use evidence from Chapter 17. You may quote a short phrase from Excerpt 2.
  3. Explain how the evidence shows perseverance.
  4. Use evidence from Chapter 18 and explain its effect on Nya.
  5. Explain why the two stories meet at the ending.

Sentence frames: “Initially, …” · “The repetition of … suggests …” · “By contrast, …” · “This changes Nya’s future because …”

Check (8 points): clear claim /2 · accurate evidence from both chapters /2 · explanation /2 · coherent sentences /2.

Teacher’s model · read after drafting

By the end of the novel, Salva turns the perseverance that kept him alive into service to others. In Chapter 17, he works on his project “one problem at a time.” This repeated idea shows that he uses small steps to manage a difficult goal, just as he once managed his journey. He also faces his fear of public speaking so that people will support the project. In Chapter 18, the well brings clean water to Nya’s village and makes school possible for her. His decision to help the Nuer shows that his concern extends beyond his own community. When Nya meets Salva, the two stories connect survival in the past with opportunity in the future.
