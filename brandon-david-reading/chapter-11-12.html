1. Loss, hope, and change

Chapters 11–12

A Long Walk to Water · Linda Sue Park Session 6 · Brandon / David · Grade 8 Close reading and discussion · 30 minutes

How can someone feel grief and strength at the same time?

2. Two stories, different changes

Our reading route

Nya: Southern Sudan, 2008–2009. The village prepares for drilling. Salva: 1985, then July 1991. He reaches Itang and later faces expulsion. 0–3 min: recall • 3–21 min: excerpts • 21–27 min: compare and discuss • 27–30 min: writing plan

Recall: How did Chapter 10 end for Salva?

3. Nya’s daily walk continues

Excerpt 1 · Ch. 11 · PDF p. 53

Nya was still making the two daily trips to the pond. Each time she returned, she could see that slowly but surely the patch of cleared earth was growing larger.

What changes in the village? What stays the same for Nya? How does “slowly but surely” affect the mood?

4. Strength after loss

Excerpt 2 · Ch. 11 · PDF p. 53

Marial and Uncle were no longer by his side, and they never would be again, but Salva knew that both of them would have wanted him to survive, to finish the trip and reach the Itang refugee camp safely. It was almost as if they had left their strength with him, to help him on his journey.

Is “left their strength with him” literal or figurative? Explain how memories can influence an action.

5. Grief and determination

Close reading · Ch. 11

Salva still feels terrible sadness. He also walks more boldly. After Uncle’s death, the group gives him little support. Their rejection increases his determination to prove them wrong.

Complete: Although Salva feels ___, he ___ because ___. Does becoming stronger mean he has stopped grieving?

6. Safety does not end uncertainty

Excerpt 3 · Ch. 11 · PDF p. 55

The camp was safe from the war. There were no men with guns or machetes, no planes with bombs overhead. On the evening of his very first day, Salva was given a bowl of boiled maize to eat, and another one the next morning. Already things were better here than they had been during the journey.

Which needs does the camp meet? Why might Salva still feel restless and search every face?

7. The iron giraffe

Excerpt 4 · Ch. 12 · PDF p. 56

An iron giraffe. A red giraffe that made very loud noises. The giraffe was a tall drill that had been brought to the village by the two men who had visited earlier.

Identify the metaphor. What features link the drill to a giraffe? What does the description reveal about Nya’s perspective?

8. A village working together

Close reading · Ch. 12

Women carry rocks to the site. Other villagers break them into gravel. Nya hears machinery, hammering, shouting, and singing. She still does not understand how this work will produce water.

Name two sensory details and explain their effect. Why does the author show the work before showing its result?

9. One day at a time

Excerpt 5 · Ch. 12 · PDF p. 58

Uncle had helped him get through the desert that way, bit by bit, one step at a time. Perhaps ... perhaps Salva could get through life at the camp in the same way. I need only to get through the rest of this day, he told himself. This day and no other.

What changes about Uncle’s strategy: the method or the situation? Explain the effect of the final short sentence.

10. What Salva believes

Evidence and inference · Ch. 12

The orange headscarf reminds Salva of his mother, but the woman is not her. Salva comes to believe his family is dead. At this point, this is his conclusion, not independently confirmed news about every family member.

How does hope turn into despair in this scene? How does remembering his family help him keep going?

11. Safety changes suddenly

Excerpt 6 · Ch. 12 · PDF p. 59

The orders were not just to leave the camp but to leave Ethiopia. Immediately, there was chaos. It was as if the people ceased to be people and instead became an enormous herd of panicked, stampeding twolegged creatures.

What comparison does the author make? How does it convey the crowd’s fear and loss of control?

12. Six years and a cliffhanger

Structure · Ch. 12

The story jumps from 1985 to July 1991. Salva is almost seventeen. Soldiers drive the refugees toward the Gilo River, on the Ethiopia–Sudan border. The rainy season makes the current dangerous. The river also has crocodiles.

Why compress six years, then slow down for the escape? How does the chapter ending create suspense?

13. Six words to discuss

Vocabulary in context

mourn: express sorrow after a death numb: unable to feel normally restless: unable to settle or relax clangor: loud, clashing noise despair: a feeling of having no hope torrents: large amounts of fast-flowing water

Use two words to explain one scene. Extension: Compare doubt with despair.

14. Different kinds of uncertainty

Comparison and writing

Prompt: How do Nya and Salva respond to uncertainty in Chapters 11–12? Write 5–6 sentences. State a claim, use one detail about each character, and explain the contrast. Frame: While Nya wonders whether ___, Salva struggles with ___.

Use although or while and two vocabulary words. Explain your evidence instead of only retelling events.

Exit ticket

독해 확인·예시 답안

1. Why does Nya doubt that water can be found?

예시 답안

The ground looks dry and hard. Her conclusion comes from what she can see on the surface.

2. What work takes place in Chapter 11?

예시 답안

Villagers clear trees, bushes, and grass between the trees. Snakes and scorpions make the task dangerous.

3. How do Marial and Uncle continue to influence Salva?

예시 답안

He believes they would want him to survive. Remembering them gives him determination.

4. How does the group treat Salva after Uncle dies?

예시 답안

They complain about him and give him little food or companionship. He wants to prove their judgment wrong.

5. Why is camp life both better and difficult?

예시 답안

He has food and protection from the war, but is separated from familiar people and cannot find his family.

6. What is the iron giraffe?

예시 답안

It is the tall drill. The metaphor connects unfamiliar equipment with an animal Nya knows.

7. How do the villagers help the drilling project?

예시 답안

Women carry rocks and other villagers pound them into gravel. The work involves many people.

8. Does Chapter 12 confirm the fate of every family member?

예시 답안

No. Salva concludes that his family is gone. Distinguish his belief from independently verified information.

9. How does Salva adapt Uncle’s advice?

예시 답안

He focuses on surviving the rest of one day, just as he once focused on reaching a nearby landmark.

10. What changes in July 1991, and what danger follows?

예시 답안

Soldiers force the refugees out of Ethiopia toward the Gilo River. The current is swollen by rain and crocodiles live there.
